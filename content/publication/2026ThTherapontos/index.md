abstract = "The increasing penetration of Renewable Energy Sources (RES) has introduced significant operational challenges for modern power systems. The progressive decommissioning of large conventional generation units has reduced system inertia and strength, thereby compromising frequency stability and increasing susceptibility to voltage security issues. To maintain secure operation under these conditions, system operators enforce minimum operational requirements that often result in excessive RES curtailment. In practice, active curtailment is typically limited to large-scale Distributed Energy Resources (DERs). At the same time, smaller installations lack remote control capabilities and are therefore classified as Uncontrollable Distributed Energy Resources (UDERs). The growing penetration of UDERs further exacerbates operational challenges, as these resources cannot participate in coordinated curtailment actions. In addition, RES installations are frequently located in areas with limited grid capacity, leading to congestion in both transmission and distribution networks due to excessive reverse power flows. This thesis investigates methods for enabling the secure operation of low-inertia isolated power systems with high RES penetration. Real-time control strategies are developed to enhance frequency stability, increase hosting capacity, and alleviate network congestion. The use of curtailed RES energy for congestion management and primary frequency support is examined, demonstrating that curtailed generation can provide effective support during under-frequency events without additional curtailments. Curtailment-based congestion management strategies are shown to enable higher RES penetration while increasing the availability of frequency containment reserves. Furthermore, optimal export limitation schemes are formulated to maximise the RES hosting capacity of low-voltage distribution systems. An equipment-free control methodology is also proposed to increase the hosting capacity of UDERs in isolated power systems by exploiting system frequency as an implicit communication signal for regulating active power output, thereby avoiding cybersecurity concerns and the need to control numerous small-scale resources directly. In addition to real-time control, this thesis develops security-aware operational planning tools to ensure robust system operation under high RES penetration. A comprehensive methodology for determining minimum synchronous generation requirements in low-inertia isolated power systems is presented. The proposed framework integrates frequency stability, system strength, static security, and transient stability constraints within a unified formulation suitable for practical utility implementation. The methodology yields sets of Unit Commitment (UC) solutions that guarantee secure system operation under credible contingencies. Moreover, the traditional UC formulation is extended by introducing novel constraints to address maximum available RES curtailment, rate-of-change-of-frequency (RoCoF) limits, and must-run generation requirements. To ensure equitable allocation of RES curtailments, a fairness constraint is incorporated into the UC formulation, resulting in a fair and security-aware UC framework tailored to low-inertia power systems. Overall, the methodologies developed in this thesis provide practical, cost-effective solutions to address operational challenges associated with high RES penetration in low-inertia isolated power systems, while enhancing system security, increasing RES hosting capacity, and ensuring fairness in curtailment allocation."
