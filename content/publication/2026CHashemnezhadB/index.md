The controller coordinates inverter reactive power setpoints after fault clearing, while its actions are held fixed during the fault-on interval to avoid interference with fast inverter inner-loop and fault ride-through dynamics. The approach is evaluated using RMS dynamic simulations on a modified IEEE 33-bus distribution network over 101 operating and fault scenarios. Compared with droop and fixed-parameter PI baselines, the MARL policies mainly improve transient recovery behavior, particularly in terms of settling time, post-fault time within the admissible voltage band, and voltage violation area. The fixed-PI controller provides the best final voltage accuracy and shortest first recovery time, but exhibits longer settling behavior. Among the learned policies, Fast MARL achieves the strongest recovery-oriented performance, whereas Balanced MARL provides a more conservative response with lower voltage peaks and reduced peak reactive power injection.
