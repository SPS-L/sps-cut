abstract = "The rapid growth in Heat Pump (HP) usage poses considerable challenges for distribution grids, yet also unlocks opportunities for more efficient, optimized operation. This paper proposes a secure and tractable method for quantifying and exploiting HP flexibility. The framework adopts a two-stage operational scheduling and flexibility quantification approach that integrates building thermal inertia and Domestic Hot Water (DHW) storage into a convex Optimal Power Flow (OPF) formulation. First, a baseline day-ahead scheduling problem ensures cost-effective HP operation while respecting user comfort and tank temperature limits. Second, upward and downward flexibility envelopes are derived by treating the combined building–tank system as a virtual battery and incorporating both thermal dynamics and network constraints via OPF. The methodology is validated on a realistic low-voltage distribution network with residential loads, photovoltaic systems, and stochastic DHW demand. Results demonstrate that upward flexibility is constrained by comfort and voltage constraints at higher HP penetration levels, whereas downward flexibility is constrained solely by user comfort. Furthermore, the case study highlights that network limits become binding at and beyond 60% HP penetration, underscoring the importance of the approach in realistic high-adoption scenarios."
